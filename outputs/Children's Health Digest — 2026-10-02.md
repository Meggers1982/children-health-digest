## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Nebraska's Medicaid Work Reporting Mandate Is Already Causing Coverage Losses, First State Data Shows**
Published: 2026-10-01 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/30/nebraska-releases-first-data-on-medicaid-work-reporting-requirement-as-expected-coverage-losses-start-to-mount-due-to-red-tape/)

Nebraska — the first state to implement what will become a mandatory Medicaid work reporting requirement — has released the first data on its rollout, and coverage losses are mounting due to paperwork barriers. The state began enforcing new rules for applicants earlier this year; the Georgetown analysis characterizes the losses as driven by "red tape" rather than failure to meet work requirements. This is the first real-world evidence of how the federal work-reporting mandate will affect low-income families nationwide.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to NYT Well, KFF Health News, or a parenting outlet as a warning story for Medicaid-enrolled families: "What Nebraska's New Medicaid Rules Mean for Your Coverage — and What to Do Now." Frame around the paperwork burden and what families need to document to stay enrolled.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pediatric News or Health Affairs Blog — first hard data on federal work-reporting mandate could shape how other states implement; implications for pediatric practices seeing Medicaid patients.

## FRAXA Research Foundation
*Clinical & Research*

**Drug Company Ends Promising Fragile X Trial for Adolescents, Adults May Continue**
Published: 2026-10-01 | Source: FRAXA Research Foundation (Fragile X) | [Link](https://www.fraxa.org/shionogi-to-end-zatolmilast-open-label-extension-for-adolescents/)

Shionogi has announced it will discontinue the open-label extension of its zatolmilast trial for adolescents with Fragile X syndrome; adult participants may continue. Zatolmilast is a PDE4D inhibitor that had shown potential for cognitive improvement in Fragile X, a leading genetic cause of intellectual disability and autism. The decision will affect families whose teens had been receiving the experimental treatment. (Summary from headline and org context; limited detail in source.)

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Spectrum News, Disability Scoop, or a special-needs parenting outlet: the emotional and medical stakes for families whose children lose access to a promising treatment mid-trial, and what it reveals about the economics of rare-disease drug development.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pediatric News or a pharma trade — why companies pull pediatric extensions while continuing adult trials; regulatory and commercial implications for rare-disease research.

## KFF
*Clinical & Research*

**Federal Government Changes How It Counts Measles Deaths Amid Ongoing Outbreak**
Published: 2026-10-01 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/other-health/how-is-the-federal-government-counting-measles-deaths/)

HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. altered the federal government's official methodology for tracking measles deaths after Pennsylvania reported four fatalities. The KFF analysis details the two surveillance systems involved — the Nationally Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System (NNDSS) and the National Vital Statistics System (NVSS) — and the strengths and weaknesses of each. The change has implications for public transparency and outbreak response.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** High-value explainer for NYT Well, Parents, or Consumer Reports: "Why the Government Changed How It Counts Measles Deaths — and What That Means for Parents Tracking the Outbreak." Help readers understand what the official numbers do and don't tell them.
- **Trade/B2B:** Health Affairs Blog, Pediatric News — implications for pediatricians communicating risk to vaccine-hesitant parents when official surveillance data is in flux.
