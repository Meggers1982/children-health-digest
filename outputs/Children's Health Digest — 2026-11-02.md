## KFF
*Category: Clinical & Research*

**New Analysis: Medicaid's Medical Transportation Benefit Serves Millions for Less Than 1% of Program Spending**
Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaids-transportation-benefit-and-the-people-who-use-it/)

KFF released a new issue brief examining Medicaid's non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT) benefit — the program that helps low-income enrollees get rides to medical appointments. The analysis details how states administer NEMT, spending patterns, and user demographics, noting the benefit accounts for less than 1% of total Medicaid spending. The brief comes as the current administration increases scrutiny of Medicaid fraud.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** For parents of children on Medicaid — especially those in rural areas or without reliable cars — this benefit can be the difference between a kid making a specialist appointment or missing it entirely. Pitch to Parents or a regional parenting outlet: "Does Your Child Qualify for Free Rides to the Doctor? What Families Need to Know About Medicaid Transportation." Explainer on how to access NEMT, what's covered, and why the benefit may face cuts.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pediatric practices and FQHCs serving Medicaid populations should understand NEMT mechanics and potential policy shifts. Angle for Contemporary Pediatrics or state medical association newsletters: how transportation barriers affect pediatric no-show rates and what practices can do to help families navigate the benefit.

## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Category: Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Federal Judge Blocks CMS Rule That Made It Easier to Kick Sick People Off Medicaid**
Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/02/federal-judge-throws-a-lifeline-to-medicaid-enrollees-by-vacating-cmss-unlawful-substantial-impairment-test/)

A federal judge on October 29 vacated a CMS interim final rule provision that had created a stricter "substantial impairment" test for Medicaid work reporting requirements. The ruling means enrollees dealing with serious health conditions — including cancer, mental health challenges, and substance use disorders — will have an easier path to maintaining coverage while managing their illnesses. Georgetown CCF frames this as a significant protection for vulnerable Medicaid populations.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** This ruling directly affects families of children with chronic or serious illnesses whose parents are on Medicaid. If a parent loses coverage because they couldn't meet work requirements while caring for a sick child, the whole family's stability is at risk. Pitch to health/parenting outlets or disability-focused publications: "What a New Court Ruling Means for Parents on Medicaid Caring for Seriously Ill Kids." Focus on real-world scenarios where this protection matters.
- **Trade/B2B:** Health policy trades (Health Affairs Blog, Modern Healthcare) — analysis of the ruling's implications for state Medicaid agencies and whether CMS will appeal. Also relevant for pediatric oncology and behavioral health providers whose patients' families may have been affected by the now-vacated rule.
