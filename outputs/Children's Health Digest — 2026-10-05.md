## KFF
*Category: Clinical & Research*

**Community Health Centers Served 32 Million Patients in 2024 — New Data Shows Who Relies on Safety-Net Care**
Published: 2026-10-05 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/community-health-center-patients-financing-and-services/)

KFF's new analysis of 2024 Uniform Data System data shows community health centers served more than 32 million patients in medically underserved urban and rural areas. These federally qualified health centers are a primary source of pediatric care for low-income and uninsured families — and a companion brief examines how recent federal policy changes may affect their financing and patient mix.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Explainer for Parents or Today's Parent: "What Are Community Health Centers — and Could Your Family Use One?" Many parents don't know FQHCs exist or that they offer sliding-scale pediatric care regardless of insurance status. The 32 million patient figure is a hook to explain who qualifies, what services are available (vaccines, well-child visits, behavioral health), and how to find one. Especially relevant as families lose Medicaid coverage post-unwinding.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Contemporary Pediatrics or Pediatric News: brief on how the patient mix at FQHCs is shifting and what the policy changes flagged in KFF's rural/urban companion brief could mean for pediatric primary care capacity in underserved areas.

**Rural vs. Urban Health Centers: New Analysis Flags Policy Risks for Underserved Kids**
Published: 2026-10-05 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/rural-and-urban-health-center-patients-and-financing/)

A companion KFF brief breaks down 2025 UDS data by rural and urban health centers, examining differences in patient populations and financing — and modeling potential impacts of recent federal policy changes on these safety-net providers. Rural health centers often serve as the only source of pediatric care in their communities.

- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades (Health Affairs blog, Modern Healthcare): analysis piece on the differential vulnerability of rural vs. urban FQHCs to proposed Medicaid financing changes.

**Medicaid Home Care Waiting Lists: 10-Year Trend Data Reveals Gaps for Children with Disabilities**
Published: 2026-10-05 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/a-look-at-waiting-lists-for-medicaid-home-and-community-based-services-from-2016-to-2026/)

KFF's 2016–2026 longitudinal data on Medicaid home- and community-based services (HCBS) waiting lists shows persistent backlogs for in-home care. The brief notes that waiting lists are an incomplete measure of unmet need — but for families of children with complex medical needs or disabilities, years-long waits for home nursing or respite care are common.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Investigative/advocacy feature for The New York Times Well, STAT, or disability-focused parenting outlets: "The Invisible Wait: Families Caring for Medically Complex Kids at Home — Alone." Use KFF's 10-year data as the backbone, then profile families who've waited years for HCBS slots. Highlight state-by-state variation. Strong personal-essay or first-person pitch potential for parents in this situation.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Hospitals & Health Networks or health policy trades: state Medicaid directors are under pressure to reduce HCBS waits — what tools are working, and what's driving the backlog?

## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Category: Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Georgetown CCF Calls House Medicaid Fraud Report "Oversight Done Wrong" — Bills Could Threaten Kids' Coverage**
Published: 2026-10-05 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/10/05/house-committee-report-on-fraud-against-medicare-and-medicaid-oversight-done-wrong/)

Georgetown CCF published a sharp critique of the House Energy & Commerce Committee's September 29 Majority Staff Report on Medicare and Medicaid fraud, released five weeks before the midterm elections. The committee simultaneously announced 13 Medicaid-related bills framed as anti-fraud measures. CCF argues the report mischaracterizes program integrity issues and warns the proposed legislation could create new barriers to enrollment and coverage for children and families.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Explainer for consumer health outlets or parenting sites: "What the New Medicaid 'Fraud' Bills Could Mean for Your Child's Coverage." Translate the policy fight into what parents need to know — are eligibility checks about to get harder? Could kids lose coverage due to paperwork requirements disguised as fraud prevention? Lead with the practical impact, use CCF's analysis as expert sourcing.
- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades (Health Affairs, Inside Health Policy, Politico Pulse): tick-tock on the political timing (five weeks before midterms), with CCF's critique as the counterpoint. What's actually in the 13 bills, and how do children's health advocates plan to respond?
