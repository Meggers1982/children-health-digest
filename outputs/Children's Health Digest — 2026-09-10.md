## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Analysis: Foundation for Government Accountability Data on Medicaid Work Requirements Called "Fatally Flawed"**
Published: 2026-09-09 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/09/right-wing-data-arguing-for-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements-is-fatally-flawed/)

Georgetown CCF released a detailed critique of methodology used by the Foundation for Government Accountability (FGA) to argue for Medicaid work reporting requirements. CCF contends FGA's data supporting these requirements—now part of the 2025 Reconciliation Law—contains fundamental analytical errors. The piece arrives as states begin implementing the new federal work requirements, making the underlying evidence base a live policy question.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** For parents navigating Medicaid coverage for their kids, this is a "what's behind the policy" explainer opportunity. Pitch to a health policy vertical or parenting outlet as: "The data used to justify new Medicaid work requirements is being challenged—here's what that means for your family's coverage." Good fit for Kaiser Health News, Vox health, or a Well section deep-dive.
- **Trade/B2B:** Relevant for health policy trades (Health Affairs blog, Modern Healthcare) covering the evidence wars around Medicaid work requirements—offers a counter-narrative to FGA claims being cited by state officials.

Published: 2026-09-08 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/08/access-to-maternity-care-in-rural-areas-likely-to-get-worse-due-to-medicaid-cuts/)

CCF's Rural Health Project highlights new KFF research finding that 1 in 3 hospitals offering inpatient maternity care to Medicaid patients are already financially vulnerable. ## March of Dimes
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Researchers Identify Immune System Changes in First Trimester Linked to Preterm Birth**
Published: 2026-09-08 | Source: March of Dimes | [Link](https://www.marchofdimes.org/our-work/research/blog/prc-scientists-find-early-pregnancy-immune-changes-preterm-birth)

Scientists at the March of Dimes Prematurity Research Center at Stanford, collaborating with University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf, found that women who go on to deliver preterm show detectable immune system differences as early as the first trimester. The findings suggest a potential window for earlier identification of at-risk pregnancies—and possibly earlier intervention.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** This is a "what scientists are learning about preterm birth" explainer for expecting parents. Pitch to pregnancy/parenting verticals (What to Expect, The Bump, Parents): "Researchers found immune clues in the first trimester that may one day help predict preterm birth—here's what it means for prenatal care."
- **Trade/B2B:** For OB/GYN and maternal-fetal medicine trades: clinical implications for early risk stratification and potential future screening protocols.

**Clinical Trial Now Enrolling: Mirum's MRM-3379 for Fragile X Syndrome**
Published: 2026-09-08 | Source: FRAXA Research Foundation | [Link](https://www.fraxa.org/mirum-clinical-trial-of-mrm-3379-for-fragile-x-syndrome/)

Mirum Pharmaceuticals launched a Phase 2 clinical trial of MRM-3379, a PDE4D inhibitor, for males ages 13–45 with Fragile X syndrome. The trial builds on earlier FRAXA-funded research and is actively recruiting at multiple U.S. sites. Fragile X is the most common inherited cause of intellectual disability and autism.
