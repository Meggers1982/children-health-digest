## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Medicaid Managed Care Giants See Enrollment Drop for Every Quarter Since H.R. 1 Passed**
Published: 2026-11-06 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/06/medicaid-managed-care-the-big-five-in-q3-2026/)

The five largest publicly traded Medicaid managed care companies—Centene, CVS/Aetna, Elevance, Molina, and UnitedHealth—all reported continued enrollment declines in Q3 2026, extending a trend that has persisted every quarter since H.R. 1 was enacted. Georgetown notes these disenrollments are occurring *before* additional policy changes take effect, signaling further coverage losses ahead for families who rely on Medicaid.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** What happens to families when Medicaid enrollment shrinks? This is a human-impact piece for outlets like The New York Times Well or a Parents magazine health policy explainer—find a family navigating coverage loss and pair with these numbers to show the trend isn't abstract.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Pediatric News or Modern Healthcare: How are pediatric practices adjusting to patient churn as MCO enrollment declines? Are some states seeing faster drops than others?

**Midterm Results Open Door for Medicaid Expansion in Texas, Georgia, Kansas, and Wisconsin**
Published: 2026-11-05 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/05/will-lawmakers-heed-the-call-for-more-affordable-health-care-by-expanding-medicaid-in-tx-ga-ks-wi/)

The 2026 midterms shifted political control in at least four of the ten remaining non-expansion states—Georgia, Kansas, Texas, and Wisconsin—making Medicaid expansion more viable than it has been in years. If these states act, hundreds of thousands of parents and low-income adults could gain coverage, with downstream effects for children's access to family-centered care.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** "What Medicaid Expansion Could Mean for Your Family" — a state-by-state explainer for Texas Tribune, Georgia Public Broadcasting, or a national outlet like Today's Parent covering the ripple effects on children when parents gain coverage.
- **Trade/B2B:** For state medical society publications or Hospitals & Health Networks: What are children's hospitals and pediatric groups in these states doing to advocate for expansion now that the politics have shifted?

**State Decisions on Medicaid Work Requirement Hardship Exceptions Vary Widely**
Published: 2026-11-05 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/05/how-are-states-handling-short-term-hardship-exceptions-to-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)

States are making divergent choices about whether and how to offer short-term hardship exceptions to Medicaid work reporting requirements—temporary reprieves that can prevent coverage loss for families facing crises. The brief tracks which states have adopted exceptions and how generous or restrictive they are, with direct implications for parents at risk of losing Medicaid during job transitions, illness, or caregiving emergencies.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Consumer Reports or a regional outlet: "Does Your State Give You a Lifeline If You Can't Meet Medicaid Work Rules?" A practical guide for parents who might not know exceptions exist—or that their state didn't adopt one.
- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades: A tracker of state-by-state variation could inform advocacy and compliance strategies for health systems serving Medicaid populations.

Published: 2026-11-06 | Source: KFF | [Link](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/9-things-to-watch-for-the-2027-aca-open-enrollment-period/)
