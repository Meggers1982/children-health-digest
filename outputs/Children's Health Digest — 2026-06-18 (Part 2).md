**Infant formula recalled nationwide over botulism contamination risk**
Published: 2026-06-13 | Source: CDC Children's Health | [Link](https://tools.cdc.gov/api/embed/downloader/download.asp?m=316422&c=765838)

Nara Organics is voluntarily recalling all lots of its powdered infant formula due to potential Clostridium botulinum contamination. Infant botulism is rare but potentially fatal. The recall affects all product currently on the market; parents should immediately stop use and check for affected products.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Urgent safety story for Parents, Today's Parent, or parenting blogs: what parents need to know right now about identifying affected formula, symptoms of infant botulism to watch for, and safe alternatives. - **Trade/B2B:** Brief for Pediatric News on clinical presentation of infant botulism and when to suspect contaminated formula in differential diagnosis.

**Medicaid state directed payments near $100B — and new federal limits could cut funding to 41 states**
Published: 2026-06-15 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/federal-medicaid-spending-through-state-directed-payments-nears-100-billion-annually-across-41-states-with-new-limits-set-to-reduce-funding-to-states/)

KFF estimates that 40 states and DC currently receive $93 billion annually in federal Medicaid spending through state directed payments (SDPs) — a mechanism states use to boost hospital and provider payments. New limits from the 2025 reconciliation law could significantly reduce this funding. California ($10.6B), Texas ($6.3B), North Carolina ($5.2B), and Illinois ($5.1B) receive the most.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Translational piece for state-level outlets or national policy desks: "Your State's Children's Hospital Funding Could Get Cut — Here's Why." Explain what SDPs actually pay for (often safety-net and children's hospitals), which states are most exposed, and what families might see change.
- **Trade/B2B:** Hospitals & Health Networks or Modern Healthcare: deep dive on which children's hospitals rely most heavily on SDP funding and how administrators are scenario-planning for cuts.
