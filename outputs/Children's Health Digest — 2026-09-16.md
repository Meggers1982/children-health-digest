## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**New Analysis: Medicaid Work Reporting Rules Pose Greater Threat to Children and Families in Rural America**
Published: 2026-09-14 | Source: Georgetown CCF | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/14/rural-communities-at-greater-risk-of-harmful-impacts-from-new-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)

A new policy brief from Georgetown CCF finds that rural communities face heightened risk of coverage losses as the Medicaid work reporting requirements enacted in H.R. 1 (2025) approach full implementation. The analysis shows rural residents — who already contend with fewer healthcare providers, limited broadband for online reporting, and higher rates of seasonal or informal employment — are more likely to lose coverage due to paperwork barriers rather than actual failure to meet work requirements. The brief follows earlier CCF research documenting disparate impacts of these rules.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to a regional outlet or national feature desk (NYT Well, NPR) as a human-interest/policy impact piece: "Rural Families Are About to Lose Medicaid — Not Because They Don't Work, But Because of Paperwork." Focus on a specific family navigating the new requirements; pair with the data. Strong for outlets covering the intersection of rural America and health access.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Pediatric News, Health Affairs blog, or state policy outlets — frame as an implementation watch piece on how pediatric practices in rural areas may see patient churn and coverage gaps as these rules take effect.

## KFF
*Clinical & Research*

**Survey: Chronically Ill Adults — Including Parents — Face Major Cost and Access Barriers**
Published: 2026-09-15 | Source: KFF | [Link](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/survey-adults-with-multiple-or-complex-health-conditions-face-significant-challenges-with-health-costs-and-commonly-struggle-to-access-care/)

A new KFF survey of more than 25,000 adults finds that those with multiple or complex health conditions — including cancer, diabetes, lung disease, cardiovascular disease, and mental health conditions — commonly struggle to pay medical bills and access needed care and medication. Challenges are most acute for the uninsured. "If the first obligation of a health care system is to take care of the sick, we are failing that test," said KFF's Dr. Drew Altman.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Consumer Reports Health or a parenting outlet's "family finances" section as a service piece for parents managing chronic illness while raising children: "When Mom or Dad Is Sick: How Chronic Illness Strains Family Budgets." The survey data provides a national frame; interviews with affected families and financial planners could round it out. Could also angle toward caregiving — children of chronically ill parents.
- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades — frame as evidence for coverage reform debates, particularly around cost-sharing design and specialty drug access.
