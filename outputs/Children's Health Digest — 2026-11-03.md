**New Tracker Shows How States Will Implement Mandatory Medicaid Work Requirements Starting January 2027**
Published: 2026-11-03 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview/)

KFF has launched a comprehensive interactive tracker monitoring state-by-state implementation of Medicaid work requirements mandated under the 2025 budget reconciliation law, effective January 2027. The tracker includes state-level data on Medicaid enrollment, renewal outcomes, current policies, 1115 waiver status, and key implementation questions. This is a major policy shift that will affect millions of Medicaid enrollees — including parents of children on Medicaid/CHIP, who may lose coverage if they fail to meet work documentation requirements.

**Medicaid's Non-Emergency Transportation Benefit: Who Uses It and What's at Stake**
Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaids-transportation-benefit-and-the-people-who-use-it/)

KFF analysis examines Medicaid's non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT) benefit — which helps low-income enrollees get to medical appointments — amid heightened federal focus on Medicaid fraud. The benefit accounts for less than 1% of Medicaid spending. The brief details how states administer the program, spending patterns, and the populations who rely on it, including families with children who may lack reliable transportation to pediatric appointments.

Pitch to regional outlets or parenting sites as a service piece: "Does your state Medicaid cover rides to the pediatrician? Here's how to use it." ### Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*[Category: Advocacy & Nonprofits]*

**Federal Judge Blocks CMS Rule That Would Have Kicked Seriously Ill People Off Medicaid for Missing Work Requirements**
Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/02/federal-judge-throws-a-lifeline-to-medicaid-enrollees-by-vacating-cmss-unlawful-substantial-impairment-test/)

A federal judge on October 29 vacated a CMS interim final rule provision that would have required Medicaid enrollees to prove "substantial impairment" to qualify for exemptions from work reporting requirements. The ruling is a win for enrollees with serious health conditions — including cancer, mental health challenges, and substance use disorders — who could have lost coverage while managing illness. The decision directly affects parents and caregivers who may be unable to work due to their own health or a child's medical needs.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Parents dealing with a child's serious illness — or their own health crisis — just got protection from losing Medicaid coverage due to work requirements. Pitch to Consumer Reports Health or NYT Well as an explainer: What the ruling means for families, who qualifies for exemptions, and what to do if you receive a work-requirement notice while managing a health condition. Human-interest angle: interview a parent caregiver whose coverage would have been at risk.
- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades (Health Affairs Blog, Modern Healthcare): Legal and administrative implications of the ruling for state Medicaid agencies preparing to implement work requirements — and whether CMS will appeal.
