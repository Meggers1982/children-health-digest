### Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**New Medicaid Work Reporting Requirements Hit Rural Families Hardest, Policy Brief Finds**
Published: 2026-09-14 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/14/rural-communities-at-greater-risk-of-harmful-impacts-from-new-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)

Georgetown CCF has released a policy brief analyzing how the Medicaid work reporting requirements enacted under H.R. 1 (2025) will disproportionately affect residents of rural areas and small towns. The requirements—now nearing full implementation across 41 expansion states plus Wisconsin and Georgia—add paperwork burdens that previous CCF research suggests will cause coverage losses. Rural communities face compounding barriers including limited internet access, fewer employers offering compliant documentation, and greater distances to Medicaid offices.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Rural parents face a quiet coverage crisis: the new Medicaid paperwork rules are harder to navigate when you live 45 minutes from the nearest Medicaid office and your employer doesn't have an HR department. Pitch to Parents or a regional outlet as a "what families need to know" explainer with real-family profiles—focus on how losing a parent's coverage affects children's access to care and family financial stability.
- **Trade/B2B:** For health policy trades or rural health publications: analysis of implementation challenges and early coverage-loss data from states furthest along in rollout. Pair with provider perspectives on downstream effects.

### KFF
*Clinical & Research*

**1 in 4 Adults With Complex Health Conditions Can't Afford Care, Major Survey Finds**
Published: 2026-09-15 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/survey-adults-with-multiple-or-complex-health-conditions-face-significant-challenges-with-health-costs-and-commonly-struggle-to-access-care/)

A KFF survey of more than 25,000 adults finds that those managing multiple or complex health conditions—including cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, lung disease, and mental health conditions—commonly struggle to pay medical bills and access needed care and medication. Challenges are most severe among uninsured adults. KFF's Dr. Drew Altman: "If the first obligation of a health care system is to take care of the sick, we are failing that test."

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** While framed around adults, this is a family story: when a parent can't afford their diabetes medication or skips a cardiology appointment, children feel the ripple effects—financially, emotionally, and in household stability. Pitch to consumer health outlets as "What happens to kids when parents can't afford to be sick," using the KFF data as the statistical backbone and layering in family interviews.
- **Trade/B2B:** For pediatric and family medicine trades: implications for whole-family care models and screening parents for cost-related nonadherence during well-child visits.
