## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**States diverge on hardship exemptions from Medicaid work requirements — some families get more protection than others**
Published: 2026-11-05 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/11/05/how-are-states-handling-short-term-hardship-exceptions-to-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)

CCF examines how states are implementing short-term hardship exceptions under new Medicaid work reporting requirements (WRRs). These exceptions allow temporary relief from compliance for enrollees facing crises — but state-by-state variation means a family in one state may lose coverage while an identical family in another state keeps it. The analysis tracks which states have adopted exceptions and how generous or restrictive they are.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Parents or a parenting site: "Does your state protect your family's Medicaid if you hit a rough patch?" Frame as a state-by-state guide for parents navigating work reporting rules — especially relevant for families with children who depend on Medicaid/CHIP. Include what qualifies as a "hardship" and how to apply.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Medicaid-focused policy outlets or state health policy newsletters: analysis of which state approaches are most likely to prevent coverage losses among children and pregnant women.

## KFF
*Clinical & Research*

**New public charge guidance sows confusion — immigrant families may avoid health programs their children need**
Published: 2026-11-05 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/immigrant-health/new-public-charge-guidance-leaves-key-questions-unanswered-likely-deepening-chilling-effects-among-immigrant-families/)

KFF analyzes USCIS sub-regulatory guidance on public charge determinations under the new rule and finds key ambiguities unresolved. The brief warns these gaps will likely deepen "chilling effects" — immigrant families avoiding Medicaid, CHIP, and nutrition programs for fear of jeopardizing their immigration status, even when their U.S.-citizen children are eligible.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Strong pitch for NYT Well, Today's Parent, or a Latino family outlet: "What immigrant parents need to know about public charge — and why some are skipping benefits their kids qualify for." Include expert guidance on what actually counts under the rule and what doesn't. Human-interest angle: interview families who've avoided care.
- **Trade/B2B:** For pediatric practice publications (Contemporary Pediatrics, AAP News): how to counsel immigrant families in clinic — what's covered, what's not, and how to address chilling effects that delay child vaccinations and well-visits.
