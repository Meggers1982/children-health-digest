**New HHS Rule Eliminating "Disparate Impact" Standard Could Weaken Anti-Discrimination Protections in Health Care**
Published: 2026-10-09 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/hhss-elimination-of-disparate-impact-to-determine-discrimination-potential-implications-for-health-and-health-care/)

HHS has finalized a rule eliminating the use of "disparate impact" analysis under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act to determine discrimination in federally funded health programs. Previously, policies or practices that disproportionately harmed racial or ethnic groups could be challenged even without proof of intentional discrimination. The change could make it significantly harder to address systemic inequities in children's health care access, Medicaid administration, and hospital practices that produce unequal outcomes for children of color.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to *The New York Times* Well or *The Atlantic* Health as an explainer: "What a Quiet Federal Rule Change Could Mean for Your Child's Health Care." Frame around how this affects families navigating Medicaid, school-based health programs, or hospital care in communities where racial disparities in pediatric outcomes are documented.
- **Trade/B2B:** For *Pediatric News* or health policy outlets: How pediatricians and children's hospitals should prepare for a shift in how civil rights complaints are evaluated — and what advocacy options remain.

**Texas Report: Maternal Morbidity Worsening, But Better Care Could Save Medicaid Money**
Published: 2026-10-08 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/10/08/report-examines-worsening-maternal-morbidity-in-texas-how-improving-care-could-cut-medicaid-costs/)

A new report examines worsening severe maternal morbidity in Texas — complications during pregnancy and childbirth that can have lasting effects on mothers and infants. The U.S. already has the highest maternal mortality rate among wealthy nations, and over 80% of pregnancy-related deaths are preventable. The report argues that improving maternal care quality could simultaneously reduce Medicaid costs, framing it as both a health and fiscal issue.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to *Texas Monthly*, *Texas Tribune*, or national outlets covering the maternal health crisis: "Texas Moms Are Getting Sicker During Pregnancy — And Medicaid Is Paying the Price." Human-interest angle with policy backbone.
