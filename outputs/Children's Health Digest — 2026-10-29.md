**Immigration fears are pushing some families — including U.S. citizen children — to drop health coverage**
Published: 2026-10-27 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/elections/midterm-election-update-immigrant-health-policy/)

KFF's midterm election analysis finds that immigration enforcement and visa restrictions are causing some immigrant families to avoid medical care and disenroll from coverage — including for their U.S.-born children who are legally eligible. Federal law has narrowed coverage eligibility for lawfully present immigrants, and state responses vary widely. The health care workforce, heavily reliant on immigrants, may also face strain.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** This is a deeply reported feature opportunity for NYT Well, The Atlantic, or a major parenting outlet: How immigration policy is affecting children's health outcomes — even for kids who are U.S. citizens. Interview pediatricians in border states or mixed-status family advocates for real-world impact. Frame as "What happens when families are too afraid to take their kids to the doctor?"
- **Trade/B2B:** Pediatric News or Contemporary Pediatrics angle: What clinicians should know about caring for mixed-status families, and how to address coverage gaps and fear-related care avoidance.

### Family Voices

**Federal court ruling in Texas v. Kennedy could weaken disability integration protections for children**
Published: 2026-10-28 | Source: Family Voices | [Link](https://familyvoices.org/what-to-know-about-the-texas-v-kennedy-decision/)

A federal court in Texas issued a final judgment in *Texas v. Kennedy*, siding with states that challenged the 2024 HHS update to Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. The ruling targets the "integration mandate," which requires services be provided in the most integrated setting appropriate. Disability advocates warn this could affect how children with disabilities receive care — potentially limiting access to community-based services.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Investigative/explainer for Disability Scoop, NYT Well, or a parenting outlet with a disability focus: What does this ruling mean for families of children with disabilities who rely on home- and community-based services? Are states likely to roll back integration requirements? Interview affected families and disability rights attorneys.
- **Trade/B2B:** Health Affairs or pediatric policy outlet: Legal and policy implications for Medicaid HCBS programs and pediatric care delivery models.

**Major changes coming to the National Health Interview Survey could affect children's health data**
Published: 2026-10-28 | Source: Family Voices | [Link](https://familyvoices.org/changes-for-the-2027-2028-national-health-interview-survey-nhis/)

Family Voices is alerting advocates to upcoming changes in the 2027–2028 National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), the nation's longest-running household health survey. The NHIS provides critical data on coverage, access, and health status — including for children with special health care needs. Changes to survey methodology or questions could affect the quality of data used to shape pediatric health policy.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Limited consumer angle, but could support a data-focused feature for health policy or disability advocacy outlets: "Why the questions the government asks about your child's health matter."
- **Trade/B2B:** Health policy trade piece on how NHIS methodology changes could affect children's health research and CHIP/Medicaid policy decisions.
