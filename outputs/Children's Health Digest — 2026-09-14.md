## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families
*Category: Advocacy & Nonprofits*

**Rural Families Face Higher Risk of Losing Medicaid Under New Work Reporting Rules, Brief Finds**
Published: 2026-09-14 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/09/14/rural-communities-at-greater-risk-of-harmful-impacts-from-new-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)

Georgetown CCF released a policy brief examining how the Medicaid work reporting requirements enacted in H.R. 1 (2025) will disproportionately burden rural and small-town residents. The requirements apply in 41 Medicaid expansion states plus D.C., and at least two non-expansion states (Wisconsin and Georgia). Prior CCF research has documented coverage loss risks from paperwork requirements; this brief extends that analysis to geographic disparities, highlighting barriers rural families face in meeting documentation demands — including limited broadband access, fewer in-person enrollment sites, and seasonal or informal employment that's harder to verify.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to regional outlets or national parenting publications (Parents, Today's Parent) as a "what rural parents need to know" explainer: if you live in a small town or rural area, the new Medicaid paperwork rules could put your family's coverage at risk — here's why, and what you can do to protect it. Could pair with real-family case studies from affected states.
- **Trade/B2B:** Relevant for health policy trades (Health Affairs Blog, Modern Healthcare) as a policy implementation piece: how rural health systems and safety-net providers should prepare for potential coverage disruptions among their patient populations.

## KFF
*Category: Clinical & Research*

**Research Suggests Correcting Health Misinformation Works Best Before It Spreads — But AI Search May Undercut Source-Checking**
Published: 2026-09-14 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/health-information-trust/correcting-false-health-claims-and-navigating-ai-generated-information/)

KFF's latest edition of *The Monitor* synthesizes research on health misinformation correction and AI-generated search results. Key findings: corrections can improve belief accuracy, but timing matters — intervening before a false claim goes viral is more effective. Separately, studies on AI-generated search answers suggest users are less likely to click through to original sources, raising concerns about trust and verification in health information.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to parenting and family health outlets (NYT Well, Consumer Reports health section) as a service piece for parents navigating online health info: "Why AI search results might be making it harder to fact-check your child's symptoms — and what to do instead." Frame around practical tips for verifying pediatric health advice.
- **Trade/B2B:** Relevant for pediatric and public health trades (Pediatric News, AAP News) as a professional awareness piece: how pediatricians can counsel parents on evaluating AI-generated health information, and implications for patient education materials.
