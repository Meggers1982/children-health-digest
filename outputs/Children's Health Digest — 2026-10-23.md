## KFF
*Clinical & Research*

**Family Health Insurance Costs Jump 9% in One Year; Workers Now Pay $7,471 Annually for Family Coverage**
Published: 2026-10-22 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/workers-now-pay-7471-on-average-annually-for-family-health-coverage-up-9-or-621-from-2025-benchmark-employer-health-benefits-survey-finds/)

KFF's 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey finds workers now pay an average of $7,471 per year toward family health insurance premiums — a 9% increase ($621) from 2025. The burden falls disproportionately on workers at small firms (under 200 employees). The survey also examines employer coverage of GLP-1 drugs and IVF, along with actions employers are taking in response to rising costs.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Parents, NYT Well, or Consumer Reports as a "what families need to know" explainer on why health costs are squeezing household budgets — and what to look for during open enrollment. Frame around real dollar impact: $621 more this year alone. Include tips on evaluating plan options, especially for small-firm employees.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pitch to benefits/HR trades or health policy outlets on employer responses to cost pressure — particularly the new data on GLP-1 and IVF coverage decisions as cost-containment levers.

**Public Confidence in COVID-19 Vaccine Safety During Pregnancy Continues to Erode; Misinformation on Miscarriage Risk Gains Ground**
Published: 2026-10-22 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/health-information-trust/recently-released-kff-poll-shows-uncertainty-over-established-safety-of-covid-19-vaccines-during-pregnancy/)

A new KFF tracking poll shows declining public confidence in the established safety of COVID-19 vaccines during pregnancy. Many respondents are uncertain about a false claim linking the vaccines to higher miscarriage risk. Separately, the poll finds public framing of childhood vaccination is increasingly focused on parental choice rather than public health benefit.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Strong pitch for pregnancy and parenting outlets (What to Expect, Parents, Motherly) or health desks: "What pregnant people need to know about COVID vaccine safety — and how misinformation is distorting the conversation." Tie to OB-GYN guidance and real-world risk data. Could also work as a broader "vaccine hesitancy among parents" feature for a section front.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pitch to Pediatric News or OB-GYN trade on provider communication strategies — how to counter misinformation in prenatal and well-child visits.

**Medicaid Work Requirements May Create Administrative Nightmares — SNAP History Offers a Warning**
Published: 2026-10-22 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/assessing-ability-to-work-how-historical-snap-processes-may-vary-from-medicaid/)

This KFF issue brief examines how states may struggle to verify "medical frailty" exemptions under proposed Medicaid work requirements, drawing on SNAP's experience with similar administrative challenges. The brief suggests Medicaid's verification processes may be even more complex than SNAP's, raising concerns about coverage losses for vulnerable populations — including parents of young children.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch as a policy explainer for parents on Medicaid: "What work requirements could mean for your family's health coverage." Frame around real-world scenarios — a parent caring for a sick child, a worker with a disability, etc. Good fit for health policy features in mainstream outlets or state/regional publications where Medicaid work requirements are under debate.
- **Trade/B2B:** Pitch to health policy trades (Health Affairs blog, Modern Healthcare) on administrative implementation challenges and potential coverage disruption.

## Pitch-Worthy Themes This Period

- **Family health costs squeeze:** The 9% jump in family premiums pairs with KFF's broader health spending projections. A trend piece on "the rising cost of keeping your family healthy" — premiums, deductibles, out-of-pocket, and what's driving it — would resonate with consumer outlets during open enrollment season.

- **Vaccine confidence and parental choice framing:** The KFF poll on pregnancy vaccine safety, combined with the finding that childhood vaccination is increasingly framed as a parental decision, suggests a deeper feature on how public health messaging is losing ground to "individual choice" narratives. Pitch to health desks or parenting magazines as a reported feature.

- **Medicaid access under pressure:** The KFF work-requirements brief and Georgetown's immigrant coverage webinar both point to narrowing Medicaid access for vulnerable families. A reported feature on "who's losing Medicaid and why" — especially families with children — could land at health policy outlets or mainstream publications covering the safety net.

- **Sesame labeling law effectiveness:** The WinCo recall is the latest in a series of undeclared-sesame incidents since the 2023 labeling mandate. Worth tracking recall data for a consumer-service or investigative piece on whether the law is being enforced and whether families with allergies can trust labels.
