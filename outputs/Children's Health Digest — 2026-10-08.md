## Georgetown University Center for Children and Families

**Texas Maternal Morbidity Worsening; Report Links Better Care to Medicaid Savings**
Published: 2026-10-08 | Source: Georgetown Center for Children and Families (CCF) | [Link](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/10/08/report-examines-worsening-maternal-morbidity-in-texas-how-improving-care-could-cut-medicaid-costs/)

New analysis from Georgetown CCF examines severe maternal morbidity trends in Texas, where outcomes are deteriorating even against a backdrop of the U.S. already having the highest maternal mortality rate among high-income nations. The CDC estimates over 80% of pregnancy-related deaths are preventable. The report argues that improving maternal care quality could reduce Medicaid costs while saving lives — a potential policy lever as Texas debates Medicaid expansion and postpartum coverage extension.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch to Texas Monthly, Parents, or NYT Well as a deep-dive on what's driving Texas's maternal health crisis and what expectant mothers should know about their risk — especially those on Medicaid. Frame as: "Why Texas is one of the most dangerous states to give birth, and what advocates say could change."
- **Trade/B2B:** For Pediatric News or Health Affairs Blog: How state-level Medicaid policy choices affect maternal and infant outcomes, with Texas as a case study in cost-of-inaction.

**Interactive Map: Medicaid Enrollment by Congressional District Shows Bipartisan Reach**
Published: 2026-10-08 | Source: KFF Children's Health | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/congressional-district-interactive-map-medicaid-enrollment-by-eligibility-group/)

KFF's new tool visualizes Medicaid enrollment by congressional district and eligibility group. Key finding: In half of Republican-held districts, at least 20% of residents are enrolled in Medicaid; in half of Democratic districts, it's 25% or more. The data underscores how Medicaid cuts would affect families across the political spectrum — including children, who make up a large share of enrollees.
