## Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

**Epinephrine Recall Hits Hospitals and Clinics: Cracked Vials and Sterility Concerns Prompt Nationwide Action**
Published: 2026-09-03 | Source: CDC Children's Health | [Link](https://tools.cdc.gov/api/embed/downloader/download.asp?m=316422&c=766368)

American Regent, Inc. is recalling three lots of Epinephrine Injection (30 mg/30 mL multi-dose vials) due to cracked and leaking vials, which raise contamination and sterility concerns. The recall extends to the consumer level. Epinephrine is a critical emergency medication used to treat severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis), including in children with food allergies or insect sting allergies.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Parents of children with severe allergies should check their emergency kits and speak with their pediatricians or pharmacists about whether they have affected lots. Pitch to Parents or Today's Parent as an urgent safety alert with a checklist for families: how to identify the recalled vials, what to do if you have them, and how to ensure your child's backup supply is safe.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Pediatric News or hospital pharmacy trade outlets — implications for pediatric emergency departments and allergy clinics relying on multi-dose vials; supply chain considerations during the recall.

**Frozen Berry Recall Expands: E. coli Contamination in Great Value Organic Triple Berry Blend**
Published: 2026-09-02 | Source: CDC Children's Health | [Link](https://tools.cdc.gov/api/embed/downloader/download.asp?m=316422&c=766366)

Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A. has expanded its July recall to include Great Value Frozen Organic Triple Berry Blend (10 oz) sold at Walmart due to potential E. coli O145:H28 contamination. E. coli infections can be especially dangerous for young children, potentially causing severe illness or hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS).

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Families who buy store-brand frozen fruit for kids' smoothies and snacks need to check freezers immediately. Pitch to Consumer Reports or a parenting outlet as a "what's in your freezer" safety check — with context on why young children are at higher risk from E. coli and what symptoms to watch for.
